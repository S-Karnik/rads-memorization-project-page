Text-to-image models sometimes copy their training data.

RADS learns to steer them away as they generate, leaving the model itself untouched.

Read the abstract

Text-to-image diffusion models are susceptible to memorization, revealing a fundamental failure to generalize beyond the training set. Current mitigation approaches often sacrifice image quality or prompt alignment to reduce memorization. To address this, we propose Reachability-Aware Diffusion Steering (RADS), an offline-trained, sampling-time control framework that mitigates memorization while maintaining generation fidelity. RADS models the diffusion denoising process as a dynamical system and learns a policy-conditioned reachability value function that estimates future memorization risk along the denoising trajectory. This motivates a constrained reinforcement learning (RL) formulation, where a policy learns to steer the trajectory away from memorization via perturbations in the caption embedding space. Empirical evaluations show that RADS achieves a stronger empirical Pareto frontier between generation diversity (SSCD), quality (FID), and alignment (CLIP) compared to state-of-the-art inference-time baselines. Crucially, RADS applies the learned controller during sampling time, separating memorization mitigation from the pretrained model.

Insight: Two steps of caption guidance are enough to lock in the memorized image. Once a trajectory falls into this attraction basin, it is hard to get out, so a fix has to act early. And because the caption is what pulls the trajectory in, RADS steers the caption embedding rather than the image latent.

Reachability-Aware Diffusion SteeringTreat denoising as a dynamical system and borrow a safety tool from control theory.

In robotics, reachability analysis asks which states will inevitably lead a system into failure. RADS asks the same question about image generation. The state is the noisy latent, the control is a small nudge to the caption embedding at each step, and failure is a state with an unusually large classifier-free guidance (CFG) norm, a known signature of memorization.

RADS never changes the diffusion model. Instead, it trains a separate, lightweight steering policy \(\pi_\phi\) that runs alongside the frozen model and decides how to nudge the caption at every step.

  1. Add a steering policy. \(\pi_\phi\) is a small MLP, separate from the diffusion model. At each denoising step it looks at the current state (the noisy latent and timestep) and outputs a nudge \(u_t\).
  2. Apply the nudge to the caption. A VAE compresses the 77×768 CLIP caption embedding into 64 dimensions. The policy's nudge is added there, and the frozen diffusion model receives \(\tilde{e}_t = \mathrm{Dec}(\mathrm{Enc}(e_c) + u_t)\) in place of the original embedding.
  3. Flag risky states. A target function \(\ell\) turns negative once the CFG norm \(\lVert \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \tilde{e}_t) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) \rVert_2\) passes a threshold \(\beta\).
  4. Learn a safety critic. A second small network, \(Q^{\text{safe}}\), predicts whether the trajectory will enter that failure set at any later step, using a minimum-over-time backup (below).
  5. Train the policy offline. Constrained soft actor-critic updates only \(\pi_\phi\) and its critics. It maximizes the CLIP image–text similarity of the final image, subject to \(\mathbb{E}[Q^{\text{safe}}] \ge \delta\), enforced with a Lagrange multiplier.
  6. Steer at sampling time. Only the trained policy is needed: one forward pass per denoising step, plugged into the unchanged diffusion model.
\[ Q^{\text{safe}}(s_t, u_t) = \mathbb{E}\Big[\min\big\{\ell(s_t, u_t),\; Q^{\text{safe}}(s_{t+1}, u_{t+1})\big\}\Big] \]
Why a minimum instead of a sum? A standard discounted-sum critic averages risk over the whole trajectory, so a brief dip into the memorization basin can look harmless. The minimum flags any trajectory that enters it at any step. Swapping in the standard sum hurts FID and CLIP and makes training less stable.

Less memorization, same image qualityStable Diffusion v1.4 with DDIM sampling. Up and to the right is better.

Reading the plots: Wen et al., Ren et al., and Hintersdorf et al. keep quality high but leave much more of the copy. Jain et al. removes slightly more memorization on the training prompts, but its FID doubles (64.0) and its images look alike across prompts. RADS removes most of the copy while keeping FID and CLIP close to the best baselines. On MemBench it pays a moderate cost in quality and alignment relative to Wen et al. and Ren et al.

What the policy learns

  • Cheap at sampling time: 2.9 s per image on an A100, versus 2.3 s with no mitigation and 104 s for Hintersdorf et al.
  • Leaves ordinary prompts alone: on 1,000 COCO captions, CLIP moves from 0.312 to 0.308 and FID from 5.45 to 5.26.
  • Works on SDXL: on an SDXL model fine-tuned to memorize 51 images, similarity to the training image drops from 0.49 to 0.36.
